# The agentic layer

> **Why this doc exists:** a traceability lookup will never feel agentic on presentation alone. It feels agentic when it (a) shows its work, (b) speaks as a someone, (c) changes its mind when you give it evidence, (d) takes things off your plate and reports back, and (e) occasionally says something you didn't ask for.
>
> This is the spec for those five behaviours in Jejak, and the rules that keep them honest.

---

## 0. The rule everything else follows

> **Show the work, or don't claim it.**

The product's credibility rests on `AGENTS.md`'s honesty rules: every figure is a range with a confidence level, and the app always says which facts came from a label, which rest on trade data, and which are estimates. The agentic layer makes the app more capable — it does **not** make it more confident than the data allows.

Concretely:

- A label changes **provenance** confidence (where it was made). It does **not** reprice the money split.
- A **sample** label is a demo. It never counts as evidence.
- Observations are **derived from the user's own records**, never generated by a model.
- The app never emails a brand. It drafts; the user presses send.

When a new behaviour can't be stated in those terms, it doesn't ship.

## 1 · Shows its work — `TraceSteps`

`src/components/journal/TraceSteps.jsx`

The identifying screen used to run a scan-line animation over the photo and then produce an answer that was already sitting in the catalogue. The user never saw the system fetch, compare, reason, or decide — so the effort was invisible and the animation was decorative.

Now the trace renders as a list that fills in one line at a time:

```
✓ I read your photo          navy · cotton · hoodie
✓ I matched it to a product type
                            Everyday Hoodie · HS 6110 — jumpers, sweatshirts & hoodies (knitted)
✓ I pulled import data for the UK
                            8 supplying countries · 2024 · UN Comtrade · China alone is 27.7%
✓ I built the route         6 stops · 29,038 km to the UK
✓ I estimated the split     makers get 0.5–4% of what you paid · I'm fairly sure
```

**Implementation rules**

- Each step reads from the **same helpers the rest of the app uses** — `getTradeEvidence`, `getRoute`, `routeDistance`, `computeSplit`, `originCountry`. The trace is not a parallel implementation; it cannot drift from the numbers shown later.
- Steps are **revealed on a timer**, not all at once. Reduced motion reveals them instantly.
- The closing verdict is first-person and its reasoning has to **follow from the data**. The share quoted is the *origin's own* share of the market, not the biggest supplier's share:
  > *"I'm fairly sure. Bangladesh supplies 20.9% of the UK's jumpers, sweatshirts & hoodies (knitted), so I routed the final stage there — that's the category though, not your item."*
- The verdict gets a beat (≈1.1 s) before the handoff to the journey, so it can actually be read.

**Phase machine** (`src/pages/Identifying.jsx`)

```
scanning ──▶ attributes ──▶ tracing ──▶ matched ──▶ launching
   (wait)      (what the        (the        (product    (hand
                 model saw)      trace)      + verdict)  off)
```

- `scanning` waits for the *real* recognition to land, not just a minimum beat. A specimen chip has nothing to read, so its wait is ~650 ms instead of 2.4 s.
- `tracing` mounts `TraceSteps` with `skip={1}` — the vision step already happened above, in the "What we see" block.

## 2 · Speaks as a someone — `MarginNote`

The design rule says "plain copy", and that's right — but plain had drifted into impersonal. A chip reading *fairly sure* is metadata; a sentence reading *"I'm fairly sure about Bangladesh — that's the category's usual supplier, not your item"* is a person telling you something.

`MarginNote` (handwritten, Caveat) is the right primitive for this: it's already the "a thought scribbled in the margin" shape of the design system. It was previously used only for basis labels.

Where reasoning now lives in a margin note:
- The evidence stop on the journey (desktop and mobile story).
- The verdict at the end of the trace.
- The delegated letter card: *"I'll draft the follow-up — you send it."*
- The give-back panel: *"a claimable balance, not a donation."*

**Copy rules**
- First person, present tense: *I read*, *I matched*, *I pulled*, *I built*, *I estimated*.
- Name the limitation in the same breath as the claim.
- No emoji. Blockchain/hash vocabulary stays inside `ProofDrawer` and the Impact detail rows.

## 3 · Changes its mind — `ConfidenceShift`

`src/components/journal/ConfidenceShift.jsx`

A care tag moves the origin stop from *fairly sure* to *sure*. Before, that happened silently in a re-render. Now it's shown where it happened:

```
ONE FACT IN
FAIRLY SURE → SURE   WHERE IT WAS MADE
that pinpoints where it was made
THE MONEY SPLIT IS UNCHANGED — ONE LABEL DOESN'T PRICE A FACTORY
```

Implementation:
- The "before" state is computed by re-running `annotateRoute(product, market, null)` — the route as it would be with no label — so the comparison is real, not remembered.
- If the label's country differs from the routed country, the shift also shows the correction (`China → Bangladesh`).
- **A sample label is excluded.** The sample label's stamp still shows on the stop, but it doesn't claim to be evidence.

## 4 · Takes things off your plate — `DelegationCard`

`src/lib/delegation.js` + `src/components/journal/DelegationCard.jsx`

A letter to a brand becomes a task with a state, not a form you filled in once.

**Stored status is coarse** — `drafted` / `sent` / `replied` / `escalated` / `closed`. Everything else is **derived from the clock**:

| Derived state | When | What the user sees |
|---|---|---|
| `drafted` | never sent | *Ready when you are — you keep the send button.* |
| `awaiting` | sent, before the reminder date | *No reply yet — I check again in 11 days.* |
| `overdue` | sent, past the reminder date | *No reply in 16 days. A nudge is fair.* |
| `flagged` | sent and already flagged by the daily job | *I flagged this on day 20. Nothing back yet.* |
| `escalated` | the user sent the follow-up | *You sent a follow-up. Still no answer.* |
| `replied` | the brand answered | *They answered — open the letter in your inbox.* |
| `closed` | the user closed it | *You closed this one.* |

Why derive rather than store: the state ages on its own, with no cron writing to the database and no chance of the UI disagreeing with the clock. The daily `brandFollowUps` automation only emails the user — it does not change the stored status.

The card shows a three-stage rail (drafted → sent → escalated), and drafts the follow-up when a nudge is due. `AskBrandSheet` gained an `record` mode so a saved letter can be opened, edited, and marked sent.

**The send button stays with the user.** Jejak never emails a brand. It drafts, it reminds, it reports back — the send is yours.

## 5 · Says something you didn't ask for — `Observations`

`src/lib/observations.js` + `src/components/journal/Observations.jsx`

The journal opens with what the app noticed, instead of a catalogue.

**Derivation, not generation.** Every observation is computed from the user's own `Scan` and `BrandRequest` records on every render. There is no model in the loop, so an observation can only ever say something the journal already knows. This is why the wording stays at the level of the data:

> *"Two of your items came from Bangladesh. Different brands, same factory country."*

— not *"the same mill in Dhaka"*, because the data is country-level and claiming a mill would be false.

The observation set:

| Observation | Derived from |
|---|---|
| *Two of your items came from X.* | Repeated `origin` across scans |
| *Two of your items are X.* | Repeated `brand` across scans — one letter covers both |
| *You asked X about the Y. They haven't answered.* | A letter past its reminder date |
| *A letter to X is drafted and hasn't gone out.* | A letter still in `drafted` |
| *You've traced N items and not one label.* | Scans with no `label_country` |
| *You've traced N brands and never asked any of them anything.* | Scans with brands, no letters |

Each carries a weight, so the most notable surfaces first.

## 6 · Empty states that promise work

The old empty state was a shop window: *"Nothing pressed yet / Start with a specimen"*, four products to pick from. An agent with nothing to do should say what it *would* do:

> **Nothing pressed yet**
> **Give me one label. I'll trace the route, price the labour share, tell you how sure I am, and anchor the whole thing to a public ledger you can check without me.**
> Start with anything below if you'd rather see the shape of it first. Each one is a real supply chain we keep on the shelf.

Same promise on the Home screen. The specimen shelf stays — as the second option, not the pitch.

## 7 · Consequence, up front

Two things in the app have weight: money moves and a hash commits. Both were buried.

- **The wager.** The story's money card now says the wager plainly: your guess is hashed and written to a public ledger *before* the split is shown, so nobody — including the app — can change it afterwards. Same line on the desktop journey's money handoff.
- **The maker payout.** Moved out of `ProofDrawer` into the money flow as `GiveBackNote`, right after the split reveals — the moment the number makes sense to act on. It sends a **claimable balance** (claim it or it returns), not a donation.
- **The passport.** `PassportSign` lets the passport co-sign an anchored record. The app anchoring a record proves nobody edited it; your passkey signing it proves it's *your* trace. The action is appended to `passportActions`, so it shows up under "Your passport" on the Impact page next to the limit and recovery actions.

## Tests

`src/lib/agentic.test.ts` (run with `deno test src/lib/`) covers the two pure modules:

- The delegation state machine — every derived state, the terminal states, the clock, and the nudge template.
- The observation engine — each observation, and the cases where an observation must *not* appear.

Both modules take the clock as an argument rather than reading it, so the same record always renders the same state and the tests are deterministic.
